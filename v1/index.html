The Battlefield

Los Angeles's Housing Crisis Landscape

Before deploying solutions, commanders understand terrain. This platform operates on the traditional land of the Tongva, Tataviam, Serrano, Kizh, and Chumash peoples. LA's homelessness crisis is shaped by sprawl, a historic affordability collapse, fragmented city-county governance, and a shifting legal landscape — each a potential boss encounter in this platform.

01

Scale of the Crisis

The 2025 Greater Los Angeles Homeless Count found approximately 72,000 people experiencing homelessness across the LA Continuum of Care (county-wide, excluding Pasadena, Glendale, and Long Beach, which run separate counts), with roughly 43,700 in the City of Los Angeles alone — a second consecutive year of decline, down about 4% countywide. Even so, researchers at RAND and elsewhere caution that official counts likely undercount the true population, and the region still needs an estimated 485,000 additional affordable units to meet demand.

02

Root Causes Specific to LA

Decades of under-building relative to population growth. Single-family zoning covering most of the city's residential land. A sprawling geography that makes shelter and service access difficult without a car. Wildfire and climate displacement adding new entrants. Wages that have not kept pace with rent. A regional median rent burden that pushes working families into cars, RVs, and encampments long before they appear in official counts.

03

Who Is Most Affected

Black Angelenos are dramatically overrepresented in the homeless population relative to their share of the county's residents — a legacy of redlining, mass incarceration, and discriminatory lending. Latino residents, particularly recent immigrants and mixed-status families, face distinct barriers to shelter and benefits access. LA has one of the largest urban American Indian and Alaska Native populations in the country, a legacy of federal relocation policy, with elevated homelessness rates. Veterans, transition-age youth, and people exiting jails, foster care, and hospitals cycle disproportionately through the system.

04

The Governance Gap

For over three decades, the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority (LAHSA) — a joint city-county powers authority — coordinated the regional response. As of January 1, 2026, LA County has begun transferring homelessness functions and roughly $1 billion in funding to a newly created LA County Department of Homeless Services and Housing, with the transition slated to complete by mid-2026. Every boss in this platform embodies a facet of the resulting coordination challenge between the City, the County, and dozens of independent service providers.

05

The Legal Landscape, the Economic Case, and the Game Ahead

The 2024 U.S. Supreme Court ruling in City of Grants Pass v. Johnson effectively ended the Ninth Circuit's Martin v. Boise protections, holding that cities may enforce anti-camping ordinances even without adequate shelter space available — reshaping what LA's encampment policy, including Mayor Karen Bass's "Inside Safe" initiative, can and cannot do. Meanwhile the fiscal case for permanent solutions remains overwhelming: chronic homelessness costs public systems tens of thousands of dollars per person per year in emergency rooms, jails, and crisis response, while permanent supportive housing typically costs substantially less and keeps most residents housed. Measure A's countywide sales tax and the state's Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention (HHAP) program now stand alongside Proposition HHH as the region's primary funding levers. About This Platform

Research Foundations

Synthesises research from the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority (LAHSA); the new LA County Department of Homeless Services and Housing; RAND Corporation; the California Department of Housing and Community Development; the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development; the Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles; the Inner City Law Center; the LA Community Action Network; United American Indian Involvement; and peer-reviewed literature 2019–2026. Flagship solution cards summarise indicative evidence and concept cards are unreviewed — verify against primary sources before citing in formal policy documents.

Gamification Philosophy

The RPG mechanics here are designed to deepen engagement with evidence-based policy, not to trivialise the suffering they address. Every mechanic is tied to a real learning outcome. The Synthesis Engine uses your exploration history to surface genuinely novel combinations — recursive self-improvement applied to civic knowledge. Gamification serves learning and never fosters harmful addiction patterns.

Cascade Collider Design

The four-stage cascade generates millions of unique chain combinations by colliding challenge vectors with domain lenses, innovation modes, scale targets, output formats, temporal horizons, and cross-domain analogues. Stages 2–4 are unlocked through the Skill Tree to ensure the most powerful collisions are earned through genuine engagement. The Synthesis Engine analyses session history to propose recombinations, creating a recursive improvement loop.
